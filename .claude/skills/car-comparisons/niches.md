# Car niches: what to show, what decides the verdict

Each car-comparisons video declares one niche (in `notes.md`). The niche decides which numbers fill the spec sheets,
the rival grid and the verdict. **Deciding** numbers (ranked) settle whether a rival is a strong alternative worth a
look. **Always** numbers are shown on every SUV or off-road segment, the subject's and every rival's (the user's rule,
2026-11-01). **Secondary** numbers are shown when they help the story, never judged.

These rankings are a synthesis of what reviewers publish (researched 2026-11-01), not any outlet's formula. Edmunds'
methodology page and Consumer Reports' weightings were not readable; treat the weights as ours.

## The SUV and off-road rule (crossover, overlander, family SUV)

Always show, when the maker publishes them, in this order and the same rows on every car:

| Row | Value (≤ 10 chars) | Label (≤ 42 chars) |
|---|---|---|
| Suspension | a code, e.g. `IFS/SOLID`, `IND/IND`, `AIR/AIR`, `MCPH/MLNK` | the detail: `Suspension · dbl wishbone / 4-link` |
| Torque | `186 LB-FT` | `Torque · 2.5L hybrid, combined` |
| Approach angle | `30°` | `Approach angle` |
| Ground clearance | `8.6 IN` | `Ground clearance` |

Suspension codes: **IFS** independent front, **IRS** independent rear, **IND** independent (both ends), **SOLID**
solid/live axle, **AIR** air suspension, **MCPH** MacPherson strut, **MLNK** multilink, **LEAF** leaf springs. Front
first, then rear. Wheel travel, when published, is its own row (`9.4 IN` / `Wheel travel, front`). Not published for a
rival → `N/A` on that row, never a guess and never a number from a forum.

## Sedan

- **Decide:** (1) price, (2) fuel economy (or EV range), (3) crash rating.
- **Secondary:** rear legroom, trunk space, warranty, standard driver-assist features, horsepower and torque.
- **0–60** only from a named instrumented test (Car and Driver, MotorTrend), labeled with the outlet. Makers publish
  estimates at best, and launch and surface change it (Hagerty on 0–60 testing).
- Trunk space is the least comparable cargo number: secondary only.

## Crossover (compact and midsize two-row)

- **Decide:** (1) price, (2) fuel economy, (3) crash rating.
- **Always:** suspension, torque, approach angle, ground clearance.
- **Secondary:** cargo (same body style and method only), AWD standard or extra, rear legroom, towing.
- Predicted reliability only as an attributed note ("Consumer Reports rates its predicted reliability…"), never as a
  number on screen: there's no free, open dataset for it.

## Overlander (off-road SUVs and trucks built to travel loaded: 4Runner, Bronco, Wrangler, Land Cruiser, Defender…)

- **Decide:** (1) payload, (2) the weakest of approach / breakover / departure (a rig is only as capable as its worst
  angle), (3) range on a tank (tank size × EPA highway mpg, shown as `EST.` and labeled as computed).
- **Always:** suspension (plus wheel travel and a sway-bar disconnect, if it has one), torque, approach angle, ground
  clearance.
- **Secondary:** low range and locking differentials (a strong bonus, not a must), fording depth, dynamic roof load
  (only when published: it is often missing; Expedition Portal calls roof load one of the numbers that matter), fuel
  capacity.
- Compare payload and angles **at the same trim**: tires and bumpers change them between trims, and options and roof
  weight eat payload (Expedition Portal on payload and GVWR).

## Family SUV (three-row; "soccer mom")

- **Decide:** (1) third-row legroom, (2) cargo behind the third row, (3) crash rating together with the **IIHS LATCH
  ease-of-use rating** (good+ / good / acceptable / marginal / poor). The LATCH rating says how easy the anchors are to
  use, so it says more than a raw count of anchors (IIHS LATCH ratings).
- **Always:** suspension, torque, approach angle, ground clearance.
- **Secondary:** seat count and captain's chairs, towing, fuel economy, price.
- **Minivans are fair rivals.** Buyer guides cross-shop them with three-row SUVs (KBB Best Family Cars puts the
  Odyssey and Sienna alongside the Pilot, Palisade and Telluride). A minivan gets the same rows; note its sliding doors.
  It is often the best "worth a look" twist in this niche.
- Third-row legroom on paper says little about adult comfort; if a reviewer sat in it (Cars.com tests each row with
  people of a set height), quote that, attributed.

## Where the numbers come from, and the traps

| Number | Reliable source | Trap |
|---|---|---|
| Price | the maker's pricing page or press release | destination included or not: say which, same for every car; model year |
| Fuel economy, EV range | fueleconomy.gov (EPA; US government) | never mix EPA and WLTP ranges; combined vs highway |
| Crash rating | NHTSA 5-star (nhtsa.gov; US government), IIHS (private, free to read) | different tests, never mixed in one row; often missing for a new model's first months: say "not yet rated" |
| LATCH ease of use | IIHS only | per model year |
| Cargo volume | the maker's spec sheet | brands measure differently (SAE J1100 is voluntary; under-floor bins); compare same body style only |
| Legroom | the maker's spec sheet | claimed SAE figures; comfort is a tester's judgment |
| Towing, payload | the maker's spec sheet, door-jamb sticker | trim, drivetrain and hitch package change them; not every maker rates to SAE J2807 |
| Clearance, angles, fording | the maker's spec sheet, usually per trim | tires and bumpers change them by trim |
| Torque, horsepower | the maker's spec sheet | hybrids: use combined system output, labeled |
| Suspension | the maker's spec sheet or press kit | name both ends; "independent" alone is ambiguous |
| Roof load | owner's manual | often static only; dynamic often unpublished → N/A |
| Range on a tank | computed (tank × EPA highway) | always shown as `EST.` |
| 0–60 | instrumented tests by named outlets | not a spec; attribute it |
